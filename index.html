THE REALITY · 08 COMMON FAILURE MODES

Disconnected systems. Disconnected teams. Lost opportunities.

Most operational problems aren't software problems. They're design gaps: systems that were installed rather than architected, and processes no one owns end to end.

01

The CRM is deployed, but the team works around it.

OPSMO · Adoption is a design problem. We rebuild stages, fields and views around how your sellers actually qualify and close.

02

Leads arrive from several channels and disappear between them.

OPSMO · We consolidate capture, deduplicate at source, and enforce assignment and response-time rules automatically.

03

Manual steps hold the process together, spreadsheets, exports, reminders.

OPSMO · Repetitive routing, approvals, notifications and follow-ups move into governed workflow automation.

04

Applications don't talk to each other, so data is re-typed.

OPSMO · We design the integration layer: what syncs, in which direction, how conflicts resolve, and how failures are caught.

05

Reporting takes days and still doesn't reconcile.

OPSMO · One governed data model with definitions leadership agrees on, then dashboards built on top of it.

06

Sales and customer journeys have no clear ownership or stage logic.

OPSMO · We architect the commercial process end to end: pipeline, lifecycle, service levels and handoffs.

07

Automation breaks quietly and someone has to babysit it.

OPSMO · Every workflow ships with logging, error paths and monitoring, plus a documented change process.

08

Technology spend rises without measurable operational improvement.

OPSMO · We define the operating outcome first, then measure adoption and process performance against it.

FAQ · BEFORE YOU CHOOSE

Questions we hear before a decision.

How we work before a vendor is chosen, and what to compare beyond the licence price.

How does OPSMO help before we choose a vendor?

We start with the workflows you need to improve and the outcomes you want. From there we define the requirements, build a shortlist, compare the full first-year cost, check how each option connects to the systems you already run, and support a pilot with the people who will use it. You end up with a documented recommendation your team can decide on.

Can we keep the software we already have?

Often, yes. If your existing tools mostly fit, we say so and focus on the workflow and integration gaps instead of a replacement. Current systems are part of the evaluation, so a change is only recommended where it is justified by what the business needs to do.

Which costs should we compare beyond licences?

Implementation, migration, integrations, training and ongoing administration usually outweigh the subscription itself. We compare licence seats, tiers, add-ons, usage limits and renewal terms, then add first-year implementation and running costs so the comparison reflects what you will actually spend.

Can Zoho CRM or Zoho Desk be implemented on its own?

Yes. Zoho One is for teams that need sales, support, finance and operations to work as one suite, but Zoho CRM can be implemented as a standalone sales system and Zoho Desk as a standalone support system. Each can start small and connect to the other later.
